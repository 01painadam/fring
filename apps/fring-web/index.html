About this simulation

Test particles (10 000 to 1 000 000, your choice) run live on your GPU (Wisdom-Holman, double-single arithmetic), pulled by Saturn (J2 to J6) and by the moons from a 10-year integration of the real moon orbits. Time starts at 1 January 2008. Seen from the north: orbits go anticlockwise. Inertial: fixed camera; the core goes round once in 14.85 h. Rotating: the camera turns with the core (mean motion n0), so the core stays still and the outer material falls behind. Moon views: the moon stays at the centre, Saturn is straight down. Moonlet: all particles start uniform in a sphere of the packing radius on a circular orbit, 180° from Prometheus. They have no self-gravity and no collisions, so the moonlet comes apart at once and shears into a strand. Velocity: synchronous (at rest in the frame that turns with the moonlet's orbit), the centre's velocity for every particle (no spin), or each particle on its own circular orbit. The moonlet view turns with the circular orbit of the moonlet centre from t = 0; Saturn is straight down. "Pandora pulls" switches off only Pandora's pull on the particles; Prometheus keeps its real path and Pandora's dot is hidden. Changing the number of particles, the band or Pandora starts again from t = 0. At more than 100 000 particles the top speed is lower (the readout shows the limit). Below about 6 h/s the motion is stepwise (one 360 s step every few frames). Drag to move, scroll to zoom.

Source code and method: github.com/01painadam/fring (MIT licence).
