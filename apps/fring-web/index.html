Test particles (10 000 to 1 000 000, your choice) run live on your GPU (Wisdom-Holman, double-single arithmetic), pulled by Saturn (J2 to J6) and by the moons from a 10-year integration of the real moon orbits. Time starts at 1 January 2008. Seen from the north: orbits go anticlockwise. Inertial: fixed camera; the core goes round once in 14.85 h. Rotating: the camera turns with the core (mean motion n0), so the core stays still and the outer material falls behind. Moon views: the moon stays at the centre, Saturn is straight down. "Pandora pulls" switches off only Pandora's pull on the particles; Prometheus keeps its real path and Pandora's dot is hidden. Changing the number of particles, the band or Pandora starts again from t = 0. At more than 100 000 particles the top speed is lower (the readout shows the limit). Below about 6 h/s the motion is stepwise (one 360 s step every few frames). Drag to move, scroll to zoom.
