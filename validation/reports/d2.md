# D2: arc error test (full ring against co-moving arc)

Role A6. Test particles, no collisions, Prometheus and Pandora, 5 years at h = 31,557,600 / 88,582 s (442,900 steps, 150 steps per orbit). Written from the PLAN and the snapshot format only; the simulation code was run as a black box (`fring-run`).

## Result

**No arc width in {5, 10, 30 deg} has an error below 5 %. The recommendation is: none.** This is the case for the eccentric core (e = 0.00235) and for the e = 0 core. The reasons are different, and the first one is not a statistical effect.

1. **Eccentric core: the arc cannot represent the ring, at any width.** The D1 wrap rotates a particle by a whole width and keeps its local state, so its apse longitude changes by the width at every wrap (varpi -> varpi - dtheta). The start does the same (the fold). At t = 0 the arc has its apses spread evenly over 360 deg, and at each wrap they rotate at -(n(r_g) - n0). Measured, not assumed (section "Numbers"):
   - t = 0: local radial RMS 233 km (arc) against 10.1 km (ring); coherence |<e_vec>| / <e> 0.002 to 0.005 against 1.000; local velocity dispersion 15 to 27 m/s against 0.3 m/s.
   - apse drift slope against r_g: 6.17e-3 deg/day/km in the arc (prediction for the wrap -dn/dr = 6.245e-3), -7.7e-5 in the ring (prediction for the real differential precession -6.84e-5). The ratio is -80 (prediction -91).
   - the core width itself (not only the statistics that a rotation cannot see) is wrong by -30 to -36 % at 5 yr: the ring width grows from 10 to 24.5 km, the arc width to 16.5 km. The width growth in the ring is driven by the coherent eccentric pattern; the arc has lost the pattern.
   - the spread of e is wrong by -10 to -31 %. Only the mean of the per-particle e (which a rotation keeps) passes: 0.4 to 0.9 %.
2. **e = 0 core: errors of 5 to 20 % at 5 yr, from the moon-phase change at the wraps.** The widths of r_g pass at 10 and 30 deg (bound 3.3 to 4.4 %) and fail at 5 deg (4.7 to 8.2 % at 5 yr, bound 8 to 12 %). The moon-driven eccentricity fails at every width: the mean e is 7.5 to 10.7 % too small at 5 yr (10 deg: -7.5 %, 30 deg: -9.7 %), the coherent forced |<e_vec>| is 40 to 45 % too small at 10 and 30 deg, and the local radial width, local velocity dispersion and spread of e fail. At t <= 3 yr the mean e at 10 deg is within 3.4 %.

## What a wrap and a start would need (requirement, not code)

For a ring with a coherent eccentric pattern, the wrap and the start must keep the apse (and node) phase of the ring at the new longitude, so that varpi (and Omega) of the particle do not change when its longitude changes by a width. A pure rotation of the state cannot do this. A state that keeps varpi after a shift of the longitude by dtheta has a different epicycle phase, so a particle near the seam has a radial offset that differs by about a e dtheta sin M from the offset the rotation gives: 29 km (5 deg), 58 km (10 deg), 172 km (30 deg) for a e = 330 km, against a core width of 10 km. A pattern with one apse (m = 1) does not fit in an arc with periodic edges at all. The two ways out are (a) arc coordinates measured from the eccentric reference orbit of the ring (the offset x is r minus the ellipse r_ref(lambda, varpi), and the wrap keeps x and its velocity), with the start drawn from the same offsets, or (b) an e = 0 reference ring with the forced eccentricity treated only through the moon forcing. A0 decides what to write in an ICR. The same applies to the inclination and the node (the vertical epicycle); this was not measured here (the core inclination of 0.0065 deg is small).

## Method

- **Runs (GPU, D+, chaos off):** N = 1e5 test particles per run, `core` distribution: a0 = 140,222.43 km, Gaussian in a with sigma_a = 10 km (a D2 choice: between the observed core width, at most 1 km, and the dust strands, 10 to 30 km), e = 0.00235 and varpi = 353.78 deg (`params/ring.toml`), or e = 0. Ring: 2 seeds. Arc: widths 5, 10, 30 deg x 4 centres (0, 90, 180, 270 deg; the arc at 180 deg crosses the +-180 deg line). 28 runs, 47 s each on the M4 (22 min). Frames every 0.25 yr with velocities.
- **Statistics (from the frames; `validation/src/fring_ref/arc_stats.py`):** per particle L_z, guiding radius r_g(L_z) (planar J2 to J6 field), epicycle amplitude A = sqrt(2 (E - E_circ(L_z))) / kappa and e = A / r_g, epicycle angle psi and the apse varpi = theta - 2 e sin psi - psi. A rotation leaves L_z, r_g, e alone. So the following statistics are added to the core width (RMS and (P84 - P16)/2 of r_g) and to the mean and spread of e: (a) local radial RMS and (P84 - P16)/2 in longitude bins of 1 deg (pooled, unbiased); (b) coherent |<e_vec>| and its apse; (c) local velocity dispersion (RMS of the 3-vector v about the cell mean, cells of 1 deg x 5 km); (d) the slope of the apse drift against r_g(0) with its prediction.
- **Comparison (d = arc / ring - 1, mean over the 4 centres):**
  - A (primary): the ring in the same longitude window and the same time (ring particles whose rotating-frame longitude is inside the arc window), paired by centre. This separates the wrap and fold error from the effect that one arc sees one moon phase.
  - B: the whole ring (mean of 2 seeds). It is reported next to A; the conclusions are the same.
- **Rule:** a statistic passes at a width when `|mean d| + 2 sqrt(sem^2 + noise^2) < 5 %` at every sample time (t = 1 to 5 yr; t = 0.25, 0.5, 1 yr for the apse slope). sem is the standard error over the 4 centres; noise is the ring seed-to-seed standard error. A width is recommended when every statistic passes. All statistics are reported separately in `d2_tables.md`. In the e = 0 core the coherent |<e_vec>| counts only if the ring value is above 3 <e> / sqrt(N) (it is).
- **Sampling noise:** at N = 1e5, SE(sigma) / sigma = 1 / sqrt(2N) = 0.22 % per run. The 2 ring seeds differ by 0.3 % in the width (r_g RMS at t = 5 yr: 24.47 and 24.55 km for e = 0.00235). Noise is far below the failures found. The large bounds of the local statistics in the e = 0 core (44 to 69 %) are not noise: the ring windows of the two seeds agree (for example 12.2 and 12.2 km, arc 20.2 km at t = 5 yr, centre 180 deg); the local width depends on the position relative to the moons, and the arc does not follow it.
- **CPU cross-check (N = 2e4, f64, binary from main 0310c3a):** `d2_cpu.md`. At 5 yr the CPU widths and e agree with the GPU runs within 1.1 % (the noise at N = 2e4 is 0.5 % for a width) and the local statistics within 5 %; the arc against ring conclusions (arc10, centre 0) are the same on both backends (eccentric core, r_g RMS at 5 yr: CPU 16.65 km, GPU 16.58 km).

## Caveats

- The comparison is for test particles. With collisions the wrong local velocity dispersion of the eccentric start (15 to 27 m/s against 0.3 m/s) would drive the collision rate and the damping; this cannot be fixed by a larger arc.
- sigma_a = 10 km is a choice. A narrower core means fewer wraps per particle (the wrap rate is proportional to the spread in a) but does not change the apse error per wrap.
- Both ICR-011 (WH-EFF-CPU and WH-EFF-GPU) change the drift for ring and arc in the same way. The set is one command (`d2_run.sh`, 22 min); the conclusion is expected to stay, and the rerun after WH-EFF-GPU merges is planned.
- The GPU runs use `fring-run` built from `ws/a5-run-models` at 9e3afed (the arc was not yet accepted on main). A0 states that main 0310c3a has the same code.
