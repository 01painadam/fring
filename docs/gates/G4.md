### K4 (pending)

**Baseline, τ = 0.1** (s = 10 m, N = 10⁵, arc of 40 km, 200 orbits, fit window orbits 80 to 200, 4 seeds, ST = stochastic, HS = hard sphere, paired ratio HS/ST, mean ± SE across seeds):

| c_φ (mm/s) | 3.6430 ± 0.0019 | 4.2261 ± 0.0028 | 1.1600 ± 0.0012 |
| c_z (mm/s) | 4.7469 ± 0.0014 | 5.4771 ± 0.0035 | 1.1538 ± 0.0008 |
| Collisions per particle per orbit (collision rate) | 1.2606 ± 0.0012 | 1.2731 ± 0.0008 | 1.0100 ± 0.0004 |
| ν (m²/s) | 0.007291 ± 0.000017 | 0.010477 ± 0.000040 | 1.437 ± 0.005 |

- Deep HS collisions (overlap above 0.2 s): 0.084 to 0.089 % per run, limit 1 % (SPEC section 8). Orbit 1 alone is 5.6 to 6.2 % deep (start-up transient, outside the fit window).
- The collision rate and the shape of the velocity ellipsoid agree (c_φ/c_ρ within 0.3 %, c_z/c_ρ within 0.8 %). HS has 15 to 16 % larger dispersions and 44 % larger ν.
- No numeric agreement threshold was set before the runs. The interim report gives no pass or fail verdict. c_ρ still rises by 1 to 2 % per 100 orbits in the window, so window means are not equilibrium values.
- Hypothesis (A0, report section 8): finite particle size raises c in HS. The effect scales with Ω d / c, which is 0.32 here (Ω d = 2.35 mm/s). It is about 2.4e-4 at S1 scales (s = 1 cm), so the stochastic model would not miss it there.

**Stage 2 design** (A0 and AJ, 2026-11-09):

- `t01s5`: s = 5 m, τ = 0.1 again (arc 10 km, 0.004075°), h = 30 s, 2 seeds, ST and HS. This is the decisive test of the Ω d / c hypothesis.
- `t04`: s = 10 m, h = 60 s, arc 0.004075° (τ = 0.4), 2 seeds, ST and HS.

**Pre-registered prediction for `t01s5`** (written in `K4.md` before any s = 5 m run), paired c_ρ HS/ST, mean of 2 seeds:

| If the excess scales as | Excess | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Ω d / c, linear | 8.2 % | 1.082 |
| (Ω d / c)², quadratic | 4.1 % | 1.041 |
| nothing (no s dependence) | 16.3 % | 1.163 |

An excess of 12 % or more refutes the hypothesis (it fell by less than a quarter), and so does any rise. An excess between about 3 % and 10 % supports it. ν should be near 1.19 (linear) or 1.10 (quadratic). Caveats from `K4.md`: check c_ST at s = 5 m first; the filling factor also falls with s, so a fall alone does not separate Ω d / c from an Enskog effect; with 2 seeds the SE is rough.

Each line names its source.

Sub-stepping stays CPU only, off, and unused in science runs.
- **ADR 0009 changed the chaos-rule test** to 4 seeds with a median rule (`WH-EFF-GPU.md`). GPU mode D in the arc has a ratio of 4.0 on the bound of 4; mode D is not for science.
- **STEPPER-DRIFT: f32 J4 and J6 bias of D+** (`STEPPER-DRIFT.md`): D+ -0.011 to +0.021 km per year over 32 start radii, C within 0.0022 km, CPU within 0.0002 km. This is δn/n of 4e-11, about 4 mm in a, and 0.62 km along track in 30 years. The fix (J4 and J6 in double-single) needs an ADR and is not proposed.
- **Outer-edge λ at 30 years is 4.2 % lower** with the rule on; a freeze-only run would show if this is selection (`a3-analysis.md`, A3-ANALYSIS PR). The 5.4 % within 1 R_H of A1 was not rerun (needs the CPU `closest` harness, which K4 holds).
- **The rule removes particles**: 11.5 % of the band in 30 years, and 21 % of the particles below 140,000 km in 10 years (PR #88). Survival numbers in S2 depend on this rule.
- **Restitution law** (`K1-REFS.md`): the Bridges et al. values are secondary; the Nature paper was not opened. The Bridges law is the most dissipative. Hatzes (p = 0.20) and Supulver (p = 0.19) fits are less dissipative. K1-REFS suggests a sensitivity run.
- **Hard sphere limits** (`K3-CPU.md`): radii below about 1.03e-4 km are refused on the full ring; GPU hard sphere is refused; CPU hard sphere cost was 3 hours per model year at 10⁵ before K2-SORT. K2-SORT cut `find_pairs` (see below).
- **GPU collisions refused with**: the arc, mode C, chaos (`K5-WIRE.md`). K5 tests cover boxes and the full-ring chain only (`K5.md`). GPU and CPU use different random words, so they agree in statistics, not in bits (`K5.md`).
- **Collision step rule** (`B2.md`, `benchmarks.md`): at 10⁴ and 10⁵ particles with W = 8e8 × (10⁶ / N), 8 points stop on the 32,767-candidate limit. A physical run (τ = 0.3, about 4.8 collisions per orbit) needs `every_steps` 1 to 3, because 10 steps breaks the 0.1 limit of SPEC section 5.6. So the 10-step times are a lower bound.
- **Stretch target not met with collisions**: 10⁷ particles for 30 years takes 26.3 h at best (`benchmarks.md`).

## Questions for AJ
