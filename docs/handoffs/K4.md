## Pre-registered prediction for t01s5 (written before any s = 5 m run)
Hypothesis (A0, REPORT section 8): the HS excess in c comes from the finite size of the particles and scales with Omega d / c. At s = 10 m, d = 20 m: Omega d = 2.35 mm/s, c_ST = 7.28 mm/s, so Omega d / c = 0.32, with c_rho HS/ST = 1.1633. At s = 5 m, Omega d = 1.18 mm/s. If c_ST stays about 7.3 mm/s (the heating and loss balance has no s in it at fixed tau and v_c), Omega d / c = 0.16, half of before. Expected c_rho HS/ST (paired, mean of 2 seeds):
- linear in Omega d / c: excess 16.3 % x 0.5 = 8.2 %, ratio 1.082 (c_phi and c_z about the same);
- quadratic: excess 16.3 % x 0.25 = 4.1 %, ratio 1.041;
- no dependence on s: 1.163 (16.3 %).
For nu: HS/ST = (c ratio)^2 plus the non-local part 0.13 tau Omega d^2, which scales with s^2 and is about 2 % of nu_ST at s = 5 m: linear about 1.082^2 + 0.02 = 1.19, quadratic about 1.041^2 + 0.02 = 1.10 (s = 10 m measured: 1.437).
Refutes the hypothesis: c_rho excess at s = 5 m of 12 % or more (it fell by less than a quarter), or any rise. Supports it: an excess between about 3 % and 10 %, with linear or quadratic told apart at 8.2 / 4.1 %. Caveats to apply when reading the result: (1) check c_ST at s = 5 m first, because Omega d / c must be recomputed with the measured value; (2) the volume filling factor D scales with s at fixed tau (about 1.3 % at s = 10 m, 0.7 % at s = 5 m), so an Enskog-type effect also falls with s and a fall alone does not tell Omega d / c from D; (3) with 2 seeds the SE is rough (SE at 4 seeds was 0.1 % for the ratio).
